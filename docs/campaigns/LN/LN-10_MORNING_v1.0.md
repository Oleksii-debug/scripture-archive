## 4. Source model

### Primary Scripture
- Matthew 27:1–2
- Mark 15:1
- Luke 22:66–23:1
- John 18:28–29

### Retrieval source
- LN-07 — local chronology after arrest
- LN-08 — Luke daybreak boundary and accusation-provenance separation

### Source audit notes
- **Matthew 27:1–2:** when morning came, all the chief priests and elders of the people took counsel against Jesus to put him to death. They bound him, led him away and handed him over to Pilate the governor.
- **Mark 15:1:** early/as soon as morning came, the chief priests with the elders, scribes and whole council/Sanhedrin held consultation. They bound Jesus, led him away and delivered/handed him to Pilate.
- **Luke 22:66–71:** Luke explicitly introduces the council at daybreak, then records questions concerning Messiah/Son of God and the conclusion that they have heard from Jesus’ own lips. **Luke 23:1:** the whole assembly rises and brings/leads Jesus to Pilate.
- **John 18:28–29:** Jesus is led from Caiaphas to the praetorium/governor’s headquarters early in the morning. Those bringing him do not enter, with the text explicitly giving the reason that they wish to avoid defilement and eat the Passover. Pilate therefore comes out to them and asks what accusation they bring.
- Matthew, Mark and Luke explicitly connect a council/leadership body with the morning transition. John 18:28–29 gives the early-morning transfer from Caiaphas to the Roman governor’s headquarters but does not in these verses narrate a morning council meeting.
- The four passages converge on an early-morning transfer into Pilate’s jurisdiction, but they do not provide an identical procedural description of the preceding deliberation.

### Disputed / limited-certainty points
- Exact harmonized clock-time for the end of Peter’s denials, the council activity and the transfer is not supplied (`D1`).
- Whether the Synoptic descriptions should be reconstructed as one meeting continued from the night, a distinct formal morning session, or another legal-procedural model goes beyond what these verses alone establish (`D1`/possible `C1` if external historical sources are later introduced).
- John 18:28 does not directly name the members of the group represented by «they» in a way that licenses importing Mark’s full council roster into John (`T1` boundary).
- John’s Passover/defilement note may be interpreted theologically in many ways, but this mission grades only what the verse explicitly states (`T1`), not moral characterization of the actors (`I1`).

## 10. Completion synthesis

The defensible reconstruction is deliberately modest. Matthew and Mark explicitly describe morning consultation and the binding/leading/delivery of Jesus to Pilate, with different roster detail and Matthew explicitly stating a death-oriented purpose. Luke places the council at daybreak and then states that the whole assembly rose and led Jesus to Pilate. John describes the early-morning transfer from Caiaphas to the praetorium, the decision of those bringing Jesus not to enter because of defilement/Passover concerns, and Pilate coming out to them. The witnesses converge on the morning transfer into Pilate’s sphere of authority; they do not justify treating every procedural detail as one identical four-Gospel legal transcript.

## 11. Reusable design result: authority-handoff boundary

LN-10 establishes the reusable **authority-handoff boundary** pattern:

1. identify the last securely sourced action under the prior authority;
2. identify custody/transport actions exactly as each witness states them;
3. identify the receiving authority/location;
4. mark the first action belonging to the new authority as a threshold;
5. keep accusations, interrogation and verdict content out of the prior-stage mission unless explicitly part of the same authored scope;
6. preserve witness-specific rosters and verbs;
7. classify any reconstructed legal model beyond the text as `C1/I1/D1`, not `T1`.

This pattern should be reused in later custody/transfer scenes, especially Acts and Pauline material.
