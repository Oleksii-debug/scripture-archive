## Acts 9:7 — companions and audible phenomenon
Sources checked:
- Bible Gateway, Acts 9:7 multi-translation comparison, including NIV, ESV, NRSVUE, KJV, CSB/NASB families.
  - https://www.biblegateway.com/verse/en/Acts%209%3A7
  - https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Acts+9%3A7&version=KJV%3BNIV%3BESV%3BCSB%3BNASB1995

Supported proposition:
- The Acts 9 narrator describes Saul's companions as speechless, hearing an audible voice/sound while seeing no person. Translation choice `voice` versus `sound` does not change the minimum proposition used by PA-02.

## Acts 22:9 — `hear` versus `understand`
Sources checked:
- Bible Gateway, Acts 22:9, NIV / ESV / KJV / NKJV comparison.
  - https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=act+22%3A9&version=NIV%3BESV%3BKJV%3BNKJV
- ESV Text Edition 2025 as displayed by Bible Gateway; its Acts 22:9 footnote offers `hear with understanding` as an alternative.
- Bible Gateway all-English-translations view, which demonstrates responsible current English translations split between `did not hear` and `did not understand`.
  - https://www.biblegateway.com/verse/en/Acts%2022%3A9

Supported proposition:
- PA-02 must accept responsible renderings that express either non-hearing or non-understanding of the speaker's voice. The mission therefore does not force one harmonizing auditory theory as T1/T2.
- Translation diversity here is **not automatically `TX1`**; DEV R02 preserves `textual_variant_flag: none` for PA02-N07.

## Acts 26:14 — who fell and who is explicitly said to hear
Source checked:
- Bible Gateway, Acts 26:14 all-English-translations view, including ESV, NRSVUE, NKJV and other responsible translations.
  - https://www.biblegateway.com/verse/EN/Acts%2026%3A14

Supported proposition:
- Acts 26:14 explicitly says all fell to the ground and Paul reports hearing a voice addressed to him.
- This verse does **not separately state** whether the companions heard that voice. PA-02 therefore treats companion hearing in Acts 26:14 as `not stated in the cited text`, not as `they heard nothing`.

## Mark 14:68 / 14:72 — first/second rooster wording (`TX1`)
Sources checked:
- ESV.org, Mark 14:68 and Mark 14:72, ESV Text Edition 2025.
  - https://www.esv.org/verses/Mark%2B14%3A68/
  - https://www.esv.org/Mk14.68%3BMk14.72
- NET Bible / NET textual note at Mark 14:68.
  - https://classic.net.bible.org/verse.php?book=Mar&chapter=14&verse=68
  - https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Mark+14%3A68&version=NET

Supported proposition:
- The common ESV/NET form includes the first rooster event in Mark 14:68 and the second-time/twice wording at Mark 14:72.
- The ESV/NET notes disclose manuscript variation affecting the first-crow clause; the NET note explicitly describes significant manuscript evidence on both sides and the textual decision as difficult.
- Therefore the game must expose a `TX1` note before affected grading and must not penalize a responsible translation/textual form that omits or footnotes the first-crow wording.
- DEV R02 does **not** decide which reading is original.

## Traceability rule
For every future high-risk translation/TX1 item, source-audit notes should record:
1. passage;
2. edition/version or textual note;
3. access/version date where known;
4. the exact proposition the source supports;
5. whether the issue is translation-semantic diversity or a material textual-transmission variant (`TX1`).
